Roadmap · October 2026

A voice for your own AI agent.

Hermes Vox is an Android app that turns your Hermes agent into a hands-free phone call. Presence is the part that keeps the call alive while the agent thinks.

Android only · free and open source · you run the agent yourself

  • What it isAn Android app that turns your Hermes agent into a phone call. The agent runs on your server; the phone does ears, voice and Presence.
  • What you needAn Android phone, and a Hermes agent you run yourself on a computer or server.
  • Where it isVersion 0.9 shipped on 21 October 2026, with a same-day fix (0.9.0.1). Download it from hermesvox.org. Next is 0.9.1: close the loop.
  • Why PresenceThree problems: dead air while the agent thinks, a voice that is not your agent, and clumsy turn-taking.
  • The one ruleThe agent always wins. Presence may speak first. It never gets the last word.
  • What is next0.9.2 is where Enhanced Realtime leaves alpha and becomes Presence. 1.0 has no date and waits for real use.

Where we’re going

Each release answers a question the next one depends on. Open a stop to see what is in it.

  1. 0.9.0 · 21 October 2026

    Can you interrupt it — and does it still work after the change?

    • Secure connections only. The one breaking change: an unencrypted http:// gateway address stops working. The app tells you how to switch to a secure one.
    • Interruption that works. The interrupt floor is measured from the reply’s own echo, so a quiet voice or a headset can still cut in.

  5. No date

Why this order

You cannot judge a voice layer on a shaky call, so the basics come first. You cannot call an app finished until people have used it, so 1.0 comes last and carries no jobs. And a store listing is distribution, not proof of quality, so it runs alongside whatever release is ready.

The mode that is growing up

What is Enhanced Realtime?

A normal call sends your words to the agent and speaks the answer. Enhanced Realtime adds a second, small model on the phone — the one that gives the call a voice of its own.

Realtime

The plain call. Your words go to the agent, the answer comes back and is spoken. It works without any extra model.

Enhanced Realtime alpha

The same call, plus a small model on the phone. It says the short opener, holds the silence while the agent works, and speaks in your agent’s voice. It needs its own download and a licence.

Why it was opt-in

  • 1
    It is heavy.
    The model takes about 2.4 GB of memory, on a phone already running speech and sound. A weaker phone should still get a good call without it.
  • 2
    It was early.
    Alpha means usable but unfinished: the short lines were still being tuned, and parts could fail. Opt-in kept the plain call dependable while the layer improved.
  • 3
    It asks for a download.
    The model is not bundled — about 2.6 GB, plus a licence. Turning it on is a choice you make, not something done to you.

What the app is now

One agent, many front doors

Hermes is an AI agent you run yourself, on your own computer or server. It has memory, skills and tools. Vox is the voice front door to it.

The same agent can answer a chat app or a terminal too. Vox is not a second brain — it is a front door with ears and a mouth.

Why a call? Because your hands and eyes are often busy — walking, cooking, or working with them.

Vox is deliberately narrow: a voice-first front door, not a command centre, not a dashboard aggregator, not a second copy of the desktop app.

The phone sends words, not audio. It may add a short note about your tone, but the agent never receives the recording.

What the phone does

Listens, turns sound into words, speaks the reply, and runs the short presence lines. It also draws the being — the animated particle shape that is the agent’s face.

What the server does

Remembers, reasons, calls tools, and writes the real answer. It keeps the conversation history, so the phone does not have to.

What stays yours

The server is yours. The keys are yours. There is no company in the middle. Your words go to your server, not to us.

Technical details: how a message travels

The app sends POST /v1/responses with the words and the last reply’s id. The server continues the same conversation. The reply streams back. On a new session the app sends DELETE /v1/responses/<id>. It checks the connection with GET /v1/models. The entity name travels in X-Hermes-Session-Key, so memory is scoped to the right person or channel.

Why Presence exists

A voice call has three problems that text chat does not

Presence is not a feature for its own sake. It exists because a phone call breaks in three specific ways when the thinking happens somewhere else.

1. Waiting is loud

Text can wait. A call cannot. The agent’s first text arrives in about 1.8 s on a good connection. A full answer takes longer. Silence that long feels like a dropped call.

What Presence does: takes the first three to six words right away — “Okay —”, “Let me check.” — and plays small working lines while the agent thinks. This short opener is called the beat.

2. It doesn’t sound like your agent

A generic text-to-speech voice reads your agent’s words in a stranger’s tone. The person you built is missing from the call.

What Presence does: the agent writes a small voice file, VOX.md, in its own words. The phone reads it aloud. The call sounds like the agent you made, not a default assistant.

3. Turn-taking is hard

A level meter cannot tell “mm-hm” from “stop.” Set it high and a quiet person cannot interrupt. Set it low and a backchannel kills the answer.

What Presence does: cuts its own line instantly, then classifies what it heard — interruption, backchannel, someone else, or background — before touching the agent’s answer.

Why not put the big model on the phone?

It does not fit. A full agent carries memory, tools and years of notes. A phone model is small on purpose. The split lets each side do what it is good at.

It would be slow and hot. A large model on a phone means lag and battery drain. The small voice model is already 2.41 GB on an 8 GB phone.

What Presence is not

  • ×
    Not a second agent.
    It has no tools, no memory of its own, and no facts of its own.
  • ×
    Not the source of truth.
    Anything that changes the world comes from the agent, or does not happen.
  • ×
    Not a cloud service.
    The voice runs on the phone. The agent is on your server.
  • ×
    Not always listening.
    It runs inside a call you placed. A wake word is a separate idea with no date.

Presence Agent You

The rule, in three lines

1. The beat may speak first — but it is never the answer.
2. When the agent’s reply begins, the voice is muted for that turn.
3. If the agent has nothing to add, the agent chooses silence. The voice cannot make that choice for it.

In code: the agent’s reply takes priority, and any voice line that arrives late is dropped.

The rules that keep it honest

It can fix how a line looks. It cannot let a line lie.

Small models drift in shape: too long, too decorated. Shape is repaired. Claims are refused.

Tap a line to see what happens to it.

  • 01
    Never invent a fact.
    If it does not know, it says so or stays quiet.
  • 02
    Never claim a result.
    “Done” is only allowed after the agent really did it.
  • 03
    One answer per turn.
    The voice does not answer twice.
  • 04
    Speak, then stop.
    Once the real answer starts, the voice is done.
  • 05
    Always interruptible.
    You can cut in at any moment.

Words

Plain meanings

Vox

Short for Hermes Vox, the app.

Hermes

The AI agent you run yourself on your own computer or server. Vox is a voice front door to it; the agent does the thinking and remembers.

Agent

The AI worker on your server. It has a name, memory, skills, and tools. Vox talks to it.

Model

A program that has learned patterns from lots of text or sound. Small models fit on a phone. Large models need a server.

Presence

The layer that makes a call feel alive: short lines, quick reactions, and a voice that matches your agent.

Being

The animated particle shape on the call screen. It is the agent’s face, not a separate character.

Backchannel

A small sound that shows you are listening: “mm-hm”, “right”. It should not stop the other person.

Latency

The wait. From when you stop talking to when you hear something back.

Beat

The first three to six words the voice says while the agent thinks. An opener, not an answer.
